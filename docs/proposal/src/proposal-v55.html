From end states
to pathways

Estimated acceptance, agents with memory, and robust road-pricing paths to a low-carbon Norway in 2050.

Arshad Akhtar Abbasia · Research proposal for the PhD in Transport Modelling for Sustainability Transitions (TRANSPLAN) · PhD programme in Logistics, Molde University College

01 · THE PROBLEM

What an equilibrium cannot carry

Norway appraises transport with a national model system that computes an equilibrium for a chosen year. Asked what 2050 looks like under a set of assumptions, it answers carefully. Asked how the country gets there, and which routes fail on the way, it has nothing to say, because it was not built to. TØI's own review describes the current models as static and macroscopic and less suited to dynamic questions [toi1819]. Three things govern the path to 2050 that an equilibrium does not carry: a vehicle fleet that turns over slowly, public acceptance that persists or erodes, and debt that is repaid on a schedule.

Road pricing shows the problem most clearly. It is one of the levers named for the next National Transport Plan, where TRANSPLAN's first deliverable argues that the plan must be realistic and goal-directed rather than a wish list [toi2172], and yet the national reform has stalled. Tolls raised NOK 16.8 billion in 2025, about 62% of it in city packages, against toll-company debt of close to NOK 78 billion [svv25, dok15]. Each revision meets a public that has voted against tolls before: a single-issue anti-toll party took 16.7% of the vote in Bergen in 2019 [snl19]. Whether a 2050 plan is feasible depends on the order in which instruments arrive and on whether people accept each step. That is a question about paths.

Main question. Which sequences of road-pricing instruments reach a desirable 2050 on emissions, finance and accessibility while staying acceptable to the people and firms who pay, across deep uncertainty, and which signals tell planners when to switch?

RQ1 · METHOD · A1  How can choice models estimated on real data drive agents that carry state year to year, so that strategic end states become pathways?

RQ2 · BEHAVIOUR · A2  Does acceptance persist once a charge is permanent, and does information move it independently of experience?

RQ3 · PLANNING · A3  Which pathways, for passengers and for freight, are robust; what acceptance margin does each survive; and which signposts give lead time before one fails?

What already exists. I have a working prototype and a first measured result. At the Kristiansund tariff, a zero-emission car with a tag pays NOK 19.04 against NOK 27.20, so each electric crossing earns NOK 8.16, or 30%, less [vegamot]. With four in ten passings already electric nationally [svv25], a crossing earns about 12% less than it would in a fossil fleet; in an all-electric fleet it would earn 30% less with the debt unchanged. The toll-free Atlantic Ocean Tunnel lets some crossings avoid both stations. Article 1 counts that share ([N1]) on the road network, so exposure is measured and not assumed to be uniform. The question is no longer whether the toll base erodes. It is which path through that erosion people will accept.

So Norway needs the dynamic layer that TØI calls for. What should it be, and who has built the pieces?

02 · FIT, GAP AND WHAT IS NEW

A dynamic layer beside the national models

The national system has three parts: NTM6, the national passenger model for trips over 70 km; RTM, the regional passenger model; and NGM, the national freight model, with its commodity-flow matrices and transport-chain costs [toi1819, ngm]. They anchor the National Transport Plan and are trusted for point appraisal. TØI's forward-looking review recommends building dynamic models alongside them, not replacing them [toi1819], and the institute has begun: a MATSim-based agent assignment for Greater Oslo (TraModSim) and an activity-based model for Trondheim [tramodsim]. This PhD joins that line. It adds an exploratory, stress-testing layer, in TRANSPLAN's scenarios-and-strategies area (RA1), that sequences instruments across thousands of futures and reports where each sequence fails. The national models keep producing the appraisal that the agencies rely on; this layer supplies the robustness and failure conditions that they are not built to give. It complements NTM6, RTM and NGM and does not replace them.

The gap. Strategic models give end states, and NGM gives freight chains; neither carries acceptance. Norwegian agent work assigns traffic with MATSim but takes acceptance as given [tramodsim]. Le Pira, Marcucci and Gatta combined discrete choice and agent-based models to evaluate the acceptability of urban freight policy [lepira]; their agents are stakeholder groups negotiating in rounds, not a population living through calendar time. Adaptive pathway methods sequence actions against tipping points [haasnoot] but treat acceptance as an external scenario. Work on deep uncertainty runs ensembles over transport agent models, most recently on a fictional city with acceptance left out [fuhrer]. The evidence on road-pricing acceptance is careful and mostly static, and hybrid choice with a latent acceptance variable is an established way to read it [hybridaccept, odeck08, borjesson16, toi2179]. No one has estimated acceptance on real data, carried it in agents over calendar time, and used it to decide which passenger and freight pathways survive.

The empirical heart. The question beneath the proposal comes before any pathway: is public acceptance a measurable quantity that can be estimated on real data and carried forward in time, or not? The thesis answers that first. The ensemble and the robust front are what the answer is then used for.

What is new: three claims, and no others

1 · Acceptance as an admissibility condition. A pathway counts only if estimated acceptance stays above a threshold λ at every step. For each pathway the thesis reports the largest rise in λ that it survives, its acceptance margin, as a result and not an assumption.

2 · Uncertainty decomposed by source. The spread in 2050 outcomes is split, year by year, into behavioural parameters, external futures and the choice of path, using variance-based sensitivity (Sobol indices on the ensemble). Planners can then see which uncertainty more data can reduce and which only adaptation can.

3 · Signposts validated twice, and ordered across modes. Each early-warning signal is scored on held-out futures for lead time and false-alarm rate, then tested against dated events that happened: the electric share crossing thresholds, the 2019 Bergen vote, and Oslo's 2019 restructuring. Passenger and freight signposts are compared, because the slower fleet decides which fires first.

DCM + ABMFive layers. Each carries what the one below cannot.

Read from the bottom: the real network and register; choices estimated from survey data (A1, Biogeme); agents who remember (A2); the same agents in 1,000 futures (claim 2, EMA Workbench); the search for the order of instruments that survives (A3, claims 1 and 3).

03 · THE MODEL

What each agent decides and remembers

Each year t, a household or firm n chooses a vehicle, how often to cross and when, from a choice model in which the instruments in force I(t) set the cost. Acceptance is estimated in its core, cross-sectional form in A1, as an integrated choice and latent-variable (ICLV) model on real wave-1 data, the hybrid-choice approach used to read acceptance surveys elsewhere [hybridaccept], extended from the negotiating stakeholder groups of Le Pira, Marcucci and Gatta to a population of individually estimated agents [lepira]. Acceptance depends on the dose actually paid, the information arm and covariates. Whether the latent construct is cleanly identified, and whether it persists, is the thesis's first empirical test; the pathway machinery below is built on the answer. The dynamic form adds one first-order persistence term:

(1) U(n,j,t) = β·cost(j, I(t)) + θ'·x(n,j) + ε(n,j,t) (2) A*(n,t) = ρ·A(n,t−1) + δ·dose(n,t) + η·info(n) + γ'·z(n) + u(n,t) (3) pathway p is admissible if the share with A* ≥ λ holds every year; its acceptance margin is the largest rise in λ for which p stays admissible.

Equation (2) is identified between the two waves, with the initial-conditions problem handled [wooldridge]. The persistence term ρ is a Level-3 extension (page 5): it is pursued only if both waves are completed, and the core contribution does not rest on it. I claim only what the data identify: a cross-sectional acceptance model as the floor, one persistence term as the stretch, and no full dynamic panel.

acceptance as admissibilityAcceptance next year = memory of last year + what you pay + what you were told.

Illustrative memory ρ and dose, shown on the plate; they are not estimates. The commuter crosses λ in 2031 and stays above it; the occasional crosser never leaves it; the van driver, with the largest dose, is below λ again from 2035.

The coupling from DCM to ABM. The estimated taste parameters β and θ are not re-tuned inside the simulation; each agent carries its own draw from the estimated distribution. In year t it reads I(t), computes utilities from (1) and chooses, and the choice updates its fleet and the dose it pays. Equation (2) then carries acceptance into t+1 through ρ. The estimated behaviour is the agent, and memory means that last year's dose changes this year's choice.

Backcasting, in this proposal. Targets for 2050 are fixed first (table below). The search works backward from them: which instrument states are admissible in 2049, then in 2048, and so on back to today, keeping only sequences in which every step holds acceptance above λ and revenue above debt service.

What would falsify it. If ρ is close to zero, agents need no memory and claim 1 reduces to a static result. If η is close to zero, information does not move acceptance and H2 fails. If the fast engine cannot reproduce the network model within tolerance, the ensemble is cut to what the network model can run. Each outcome is reported whichever way it falls; the thesis does not predict the future but looks for paths that hold across many.

Tools, and my starting point

Python throughout: Biogeme for estimation, the EMA Workbench for the ensemble, search and scenario discovery, OSMnx and NetworkX for networks, OR-Tools for routing checks, and NGM elasticities for the freight chains. My foundation is a logistics master from this faculty and a background in operations research and programming. I have not estimated choice models professionally and do not claim to. As a reproducibility check I estimated a standard mixed logit on the public Swissmetro stated-preference data in Biogeme (N = 6,768, 1,000 Halton draws). It reproduces the published Biogeme normal-mixture reference: final log-likelihood −5214.9 against about −5214.8 [evdcm]. 04 · CASE AND DESIGN

Kristiansund, where acceptance is live

Why Kristiansund. Bypakke Kristiansund began collecting on 1 June 2026 at two rv70 stations, Nordsundbrua and Omsundbrua, charging in both directions. About 70% of the package is financed by tolls over up to 15 years [bypakke, innst337]. A car pays NOK 34, or 27.20 with a tag, or 19.04 if it is zero-emission with a tag [vegamot]. The city council approved the basis 26 to 19 and the county 33 to 14, so acceptance here is contested and still live.

Exposure is not uniform. On the town's road network, Nordsundbrua splits Kristiansund in two. Nordlandet holds 32% of homes and 39% of workplaces, and inside the town its only road to the other half is the bridge. Without the bridge the road graph falls into 6,580 and 4,398 junctions, so 48% of all junction pairs have no road connection. The Atlantic Ocean Tunnel (Atlanterhavstunnelen) has been toll-free since 2020, so some trips have a free alternative. Article 1 counts the share of trips that avoid both stations ([N1]), which gives the study a measured dose and avoids assuming that everyone pays.

Where freight enters. The town sits on the Møre og Romsdal west-coast corridor, where the Ferjefri E39 programme and NGM both treat coastal short-sea shipping as the competing mode for freight bound for Oslo and the continent [ngm]. Freight is estimated here and not borrowed: a short carrier stated-choice block gives the mode-and-timing response of hauliers to a distance charge, and the NGM chains carry that response to the network. The heavy fleet turns over slowly, and the sea alternative needs volume to stay viable. A pathway that charges road freight too late therefore closes the modal-shift window and locks emissions in. The thesis reports the year by which the freight signpost must fire. It is expected to fire before the passenger signpost; the design tests that expectation and does not assume it as a result. Without freight, that finding and the earliest switch in the robust pathways would disappear. Molde, which has no package, is the passenger comparison town.

Survey: three blocks, Bayesian D-efficient design

Acceptance (households): information is randomised in three arms, personal cost as numbers, purpose as a short narrative of what the package builds, and a control. The arms test the format of information as well as its content.

Passenger stated choice: vehicle at the next replacement, crossing frequency and time, under tariff and discount levels inside the national ranges [toi2179].

Carrier stated choice (firms and hauliers on the corridor): road against coastal short-sea, route and timing under a distance charge. One wave, a small targeted sample. This is the estimated freight element, anchored to NGM for the network.

Kristiansund carries the household and carrier design; Molde carries the acceptance block. Wave 1 and the carrier block are fielded in month 6, wave 2 in month 18 with the same households. The planning sample is 1,428 households in Kristiansund and 714 in Molde, reported as a minimum detectable effect, with a small carrier sample sized for the main mode-choice effect and allowance for attrition.

Hypotheses, and a freight floor

H1 Acceptance persists once a charge is permanent (ρ > 0), tested as within-person change between waves against dose.

H2 Information moves acceptance independently of experience (η ≠ 0), tested by the randomised arms. This turns the Norwegian correlation between information and attitude [odeck08] into a causal estimate.

H3 A distance charge shifts carrier mode choice toward short-sea above a threshold rate, estimated from the carrier block.

The design separates persistence from information with an experiment and does not rely on a before-period that nobody collected.

Freight fallback. If the carrier sample is under-powered, the freight arm reverts to NGM mode-split elasticities with a reported sensitivity on the key elasticity. The freight signpost is then a modelled consequence and not an estimate, and the thesis ladder absorbs the change without losing a complete dissertation.

05 · PLAN, ROBUSTNESS AND RECEIPTS

The robust front, and how it finishes in three years

The search, priced. A3 is a search and not a catalogue. It chooses the switch-on year and level of each instrument to maximise the share of 10³ futures that meet all three 2050 targets and to minimise maximum regret. The constraints are acceptance at or above λ every year, revenue at or above debt service, and the accessibility floor. This is a many-objective robust problem, solved with ε-NSGA-II over the ensemble, with PRIM naming the futures in which the robust set still breaks [mordm, prim]. Robustness is the quantity optimised, following the stability-metrics line of Shaabani, Hvattum, Hoff and Laporte [shaabani]. The enumerable core counts the ordered switch-on sequences of four of the five instruments: 4 + 36 + 216 + 648 = 904. The full search ranges over switch-years and levels for all five instruments, on the order of 10⁴ candidate pathways, each scored against a Latin-hypercube sample of futures. The surviving robust set is re-checked on the full 10³ futures. In all, that is a few million fast-engine evaluations, about 40 to 150 CPU-hours, run in parallel.

robustness · regret · receiptsRobust is not optimal. It is the path that still works when the future refuses to cooperate.

Prototype: all 904 ordered sequences against 1,000 futures; 31 reach the target in 60% of futures or more, 337 in 40–60%, and 536 fail. D, a distance charge alone, reaches 67%. E, earmarking first and then a distance charge at 50% electric, survives the widest shift in λ, a margin of +0.07. The strip marks the electric share at which each instrument of a chosen path switches on. Parameters assumed until A1 and A2 estimate them.

A thesis that survives a failed survey

Level 1, no new data: A1 on national ranges and A3 on NGM elasticities, freight included, are a thesis. Level 2, wave 1 and the carrier block: adds H2, a cross-sectional A2 and estimated freight (H3). Level 3, both household waves: adds H1 and the dynamic persistence term, the stretch. Each level is a complete dissertation, and the survey raises its ceiling. The guaranteed spine is my own survey, under my control; the national microdata and model runs are complements, not dependencies. The language-model coding of the public record moves to the portfolio as supporting continuity.

Why here, and dissemination

TRANSPLAN's backcasting and its call for realistic, goal-directed plans [toi2172]; Molde's strength in stated choice and transport economics, and the DCM and ABM tradition in the TRG, where I would be supervised; and a Logistics programme in which freight vehicle classes belong. Partners are possibilities and not agreements: TØI for the national experiment's ranges and NGM, CICERO for climate-policy acceptance, the Norwegian Computing Center for surrogate methods. Output: three journal articles (A1 to A3), one Norwegian-language piece in Samferdsel in year 3, and presentation at the annual NORS (Norwegian Operational Research Society) conference, where I have presented before.

The fast engine's fidelity to the network model is measured. On the real Molde and Kristiansund graphs it reproduces network car travel times within about 15% mean absolute error (r 0.73 to 0.84), underestimating total vehicle-minutes by roughly 14%, at three to four orders of magnitude less compute [evfid]. Above a stated tolerance, the future sample is widened and the pathway set reduced. A dynamic cross-check against MATSim (Molde, 4,000 agents, 20 iterations) points the same way: at free flow the fast engine matches MATSim car travel times to about 19% per zone pair (r 0.74), and the static assignment matches MATSim to 6% (r 0.999). Under a tenfold capacity squeeze the free-flow engine breaks down, as such engines do, which is why robust pathways are re-checked on the network model [evfid]. Futures vary vehicle costs, incomes, energy prices, the timing of national reform and the threshold λ, within ranges anchored on observed votes. Passenger and freight vehicles carry class-specific load factors and empty running from NGM, which makes this a logistics question as well as a passenger one.
