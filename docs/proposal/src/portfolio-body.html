chapter 1The stock problem

Norway won the showroom. The street has not caught up.

Picture one household in Molde in 2025. They drive a 2014 diesel that still runs. The new car they will buy is almost certainly electric: 95.9% of new cars sold in Norway in 2025 wereofv. But they will buy it when the diesel dies or the family changes, not when a national plan would like them to. Multiply that household by every driveway in the country and you have the reason the fleet lags the showroom by well over a decade.

That lag is the part of the transition national models do not describe. TØI’s review of models for backcasting says RTM and NTM6 compute partial equilibria but “say nothing about the path to a new equilibrium”toi2051. The National Transport Plan asks for methods that can steer toward 2050ntp, and the methods assignment admits today’s models suit continued policy, not deep cutsoppdrag. A backcast that cannot see the path cannot tell a planner whether 2030 is on track.

The path also carries the politics. As the fleet turns electric, fuel tax revenue falls, and the distance-based charge meant to replace it has stalled between the state and the municipalitieswps. Who accepts a charge depends on the car they own and the alternatives they have. So the same household that decides the fleet also decides whether the policy survives. That is why the engine is built from households.

chapter 3Two towns and their networks

Molde and Kristiansund are case studies, not a national sample. They are car-dependent, the instruments that matter (tolls, bus service, parking, cycling) are decided locally, and their road networks concentrate travel on a few links, which makes policy effects visible. Molde is the main case where preferences are estimated. Kristiansund is the transfer case, where the Molde model is applied without re-estimation.

The same network already answers logistics questions

The road graph that carries commuters in the engine is the one a delivery van drives. I built four plates on it for Molde to show the tools are not tied to commuting. Read in order, they go from where the network is fragile, to what breaking it costs, to where to stand to serve the town, to how to drive it once you stand there. This is the bridge to article 3’s freight test and to the logistics programme the PhD sits in.

Free-flow drive times from OpenStreetMap speeds; no turn penalties, signals or congestion; flat terrain. Scripts in skills/visual-narrative/scripts.

chapter 4Inside the engine

Four layers, each built by one article on top of the one below. This chapter writes down what each layer computes in the prototype, and what changes when the article replaces it.

LAYER 0 · PREFERENCES · BUILT IN A1

For person n choosing mode j among car, electric car, bus and bike:

Vnj = ASCj + βtime · timenj + βcost · costnj + βwait · waitnj,    Pnj = eVnj / Σk eVnk

Value of time is 60 βtime/βcost NOK per hour. In the prototype a synthetic survey of 800 respondents with eight tasks each is simulated from known parameters and estimated twice, with Biogeme and with an independent maximum-likelihood code. The two agree to 1.5 × 10−6, and every parameter is recovered within one standard error of its true value. The recovered value of time is 142 NOK per hour against a true 144.

What A1 changes. Real stated-choice data in three blocks (mode, next car, votes on charging packages), mixed and latent-class logit so preferences vary across people, and a hybrid choice model for trust. Each respondent completes two of the three blocks with four tasks in each, so every block has about 530 respondents, and the sample size follows from the S-estimates of the efficient design, random-parameter spreads included. Stated replacement timing is estimated jointly with revealed replacement from the register.

LAYER 1 · NETWORK · BUILT IN A2

OpenStreetMap road graphs for both towns, filtered to drivable ways, projected, with speeds and travel times on every edge. Paths are computed on the graph, never as straight lines. The prototype engine still uses three zones with a congestion curve each; A2 moves trips onto the graph with zone data and trip lengths taken from the regional model RTM, so results stay comparable with it.

LAYER 2 · HOUSEHOLDS · BUILT IN A2

Each year every car owner may replace the car. In the prototype that is a constant yearly rate, 1 / lifetime; the new car is electric with probability

P(EV) = 1 / (1 + e−u),    u = 0.5 − 0.03 · price gap + 0.08 · running-cost saving + φ · zone EV share

where the price gap closes to parity by an uncertain year, the saving includes any toll exemption, and φ is a peer effect. Agents then choose modes with the layer-0 utilities; car times follow a congestion curve solved by successive averages. Welfare is the logsum divided by −βcost, and each agent carries the subsidies it receives and the tolls it pays.

What A2 changes. Replacement depends on car age and household events; the price gap and the purchase model are calibrated to the register so the fleet turns as fast as Norway’s does; acceptance is carried per household and shifts with experience; vehicle and battery production enter as an emission at purchase; repeated runs separate chance from preference error.

LAYER 3 · FUTURES AND PLANS · BUILT IN A3

Nine uncertain inputs span the futures and five levers span the plans. In the prototype both are Latin hypercube samples: 60 futures and 24 packages plus a no-policy reference.

A package p is robust in proportion to the futures in which commuting CO₂ in 2050 falls to the target:

R(p) = |{ f ∈ F : CO₂2050(p, f) ≤ τ }| / |F|,    p* = argmax R(p)  s.t.  medianf A(p, f) ≥ ½,   C(p) ≤ B

The prototype applies the acceptance constraint only; the budget B will be set with the county from its transport budget. Some prototype levers, such as purchase subsidies and toll exemptions for electric cars, are instruments Norway is now phasing out; A3 replaces them with the instruments debated in 2026, distance-based charging first.

What A3 changes. About 104 futures anchored on the national strategiestoi2119, packages screened as a 102 Latin hypercube and then refined by a many-objective search in the best regionmordm, τ set against the Climate Change Act, which implies about 5 to 10% of 2025 rather than the prototype’s looser 20%, reported at 5, 10 and 20%, regret and stability beside robustnesshoff, and a run budget: one prototype run takes about 0.1 s with 1,000 agents, so the full design is costed before it is launched and a surrogate is fitted if one full-scale run passes ten seconds.

chapter 5What the prototype found

1,500 runs: 24 packages and a no-policy reference, each in the same 60 futures, on synthetic behaviour. The numbers show what each article will produce, not what will happen in Norway. Four results already shape the plan.

chapter 7Rerun it yourself

No number in the proposal or this portfolio needs to be taken on trust. Four commands rebuild them from a clean checkout, and the tests run on every change.

1
git clone https://github.com/Astheno-sphere/computational_Logistics
cd computational_Logistics

The code, the data scripts and the documents live together.

2
pip install -r requirements-lock.txt

Exact package versions, so a rerun next year gives the same numbers.

3
python examples/transplan_study.py
python docs/proposal/make_data.py

The survey, the estimation, the 1,500 runs, the robustness table and every plate number. Asserts stop the build if the ensemble drifts.

4
python -m pytest -q tests

87 tests across the tools; the same suite runs in continuous integration on every push.

The repository separates what runs, what is tested and what is planned, keeps a claims policy that ties every number to a script or a source, and lists its limitations on the front page.

chapter 8Three years, and the limits

If the survey slips. If fieldwork runs past month 9, A1 is submitted on the pilot and panel sample, and the postal wave feeds A2. Recruitment combines a Norwegian web panel with postal invitations drawn from the population register, so coverage does not depend on one channel.

If data access slips. Statistics Norway commuting tables stand in for the travel survey; register extracts are requested from the Norwegian Public Roads Administration in month 1, at zone level, so no individual records are needed.

If run time explodes. The design is costed from measured run times before launch, and a surrogate is fitted if needed, on national computing resources.

What this work does not do

  • Every behavioural number in this portfolio is synthetic until A1. The prototype shows the chain works, not what Norwegians prefer.
  • The prototype fleet turns more slowly than Norway’s and is not yet calibrated; its EV shares are not forecasts.
  • The prototype applies acceptance as a self-interest proxy and does not yet apply the budget constraint.
  • The prototype target, commuting CO₂ in 2050 at most 20% of 2025, is looser than the 5 to 10% the Climate Change Act implies; every prototype number would be harder to reach under the law.
  • Scope is commuting and other regular car travel by residents of two towns, translated to national targets; it is not a national model and does not replace NTM6 or RTM.
  • The freight plates use free-flow times without congestion or turn penalties; the freight test in A3 is a test of the method, not a freight demand model.

The fleet that will drive in 2035 is being bought now. I have built the first version of the instrument to steer it. With TRANSPLAN’s data, Molde’s supervision and three years, it becomes one that planners can trust.
