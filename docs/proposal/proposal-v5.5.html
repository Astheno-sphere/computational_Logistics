the model gives one 2050 — a household lives a sequence

From end states
to pathways

Estimated acceptance, agents with memory, and robust road-pricing paths to a low-carbon Norway in 2050.

Arshad Akhtar Abbasia · PhD in Transport Modelling for Sustainability Transitions (TRANSPLAN) · PhD programme in Logistics, Molde University College

01 · THE PROBLEM

What an equilibrium cannot carry

Norway appraises transport with a national model system that computes an equilibrium for a chosen year. Asked what 2050 looks like under a set of assumptions, it answers carefully. Asked how the country gets there, and which routes fail on the way, it is silent by construction. TØI's own review says as much: the current models are static and macroscopic, and ill-suited to dynamic questions [toi1819]. The path to 2050 is governed by what an equilibrium does not hold: a vehicle fleet that turns over slowly, public acceptance that persists or erodes, and debt repaid on a schedule.

Road pricing shows the problem in its sharpest form. It is among the levers named for the next National Transport Plan, where TRANSPLAN's first deliverable argues the plan must be realistic and goal-directed rather than a wish list [toi2172] — yet the national reform has stalled. Tolls raised NOK 16.8 billion in 2025, about 62% in city packages, against toll-company debt near NOK 78 billion [svv25, dok15]. Every revision meets a public that has voted against tolls before: a single-issue anti-toll party took 16.7% in Bergen in 2019 [snl19]. Whether a 2050 plan is feasible depends on the order in which instruments arrive and whether people accept each step. That is a question about paths.

Main question. Which sequences of road-pricing instruments reach a desirable 2050 on emissions, finance and accessibility while staying acceptable to the people and firms who pay, across deep uncertainty — and which signals tell planners when to switch?

RQ1 · METHOD · A1  How can choice models estimated on real data drive agents that carry state year to year, so that strategic end states become pathways?

RQ2 · BEHAVIOUR · A2  Does acceptance persist once a charge is permanent, and does information move it independently of experience?

RQ3 · PLANNING · A3  Which pathways, for passengers and for freight, are robust; what acceptance margin does each survive; and which signposts give lead time before one fails?

What already exists. A working prototype gives a first measured result: at the Kristiansund tariff a zero-emission car with a tag pays NOK 19.04 against NOK 27.20, so each electric crossing earns NOK 8.16 — 30% — less [vegamot]. With four in ten passings already electric nationally [svv25], a crossing earns about 12% less than in a fossil fleet today; in an all-electric fleet, 30% less, with the debt unchanged. On the road network, the toll-free Atlantic Tunnel lets some crossings avoid both stations; Article 1 counts that share ([N1]), so exposure is measured, not assumed uniform. The question is no longer whether the base erodes, but which path through that erosion people will accept.

02 · FIT, GAP, AND WHAT IS NEW

A dynamic layer beside the national models

Norway's national system has three parts a candidate must name: NTM6, the national passenger model for trips over 70 km; RTM, the regional passenger model; and NGM, the national freight model, with its commodity-flow matrices and transport-chain costs [toi1819, ngm]. These anchor the National Transport Plan and are trusted for point appraisal. TØI's forward-looking review recommends not replacing them but building dynamic models alongside them [toi1819]; the institute has begun, with a MATSim-based agent assignment for Greater Oslo (tramodsim) and an activity-based model for Trondheim [tramodsim]. This PhD joins that line. It supplies the exploratory, stress-testing layer — it sits in TRANSPLAN's scenarios-and-strategies area (RA1) — that sequences instruments across thousands of futures and reports where each sequence fails, so the national models keep producing the appraisal the agencies rely on while this layer supplies the robustness and failure conditions they are not built to give. It complements NTM6, RTM and NGM; it never replaces them.

The gap. Strategic models give end states and NGM gives freight chains; neither carries acceptance. Norwegian agent work assigns traffic with MATSim but takes acceptance as given [tramodsim]. Le Pira, Marcucci and Gatta integrated discrete choice and agent-based models to evaluate the acceptability of urban freight policy [lepira]; their agents are stakeholder groups negotiating in rounds, not a population living through calendar time. Adaptive pathway methods sequence actions against tipping points [dapp, haasnoot] but treat acceptance as an external scenario. The deep-uncertainty frontier runs ensembles over transport agent models, most recently on a fictional city with acceptance left out [fuhrer]. Acceptance evidence is careful but mostly static, and hybrid choice with a latent acceptance variable is an established way to read it [hybridaccept, odeck08, borjesson16, toi2179]. Nobody has estimated acceptance on real data, carried it in agents over calendar time, and let it decide which passenger and freight pathways survive.

DCM + ABM integration

What is new — three claims, and no others

1 · Acceptance as an admissibility condition. A pathway counts only if estimated acceptance stays above a threshold λ at every step; for each pathway the thesis reports the largest rise in λ it survives — its acceptance margin — as a result, not an assumption.

2 · Uncertainty decomposed by source. The spread in 2050 outcomes is split, year by year, into behavioural parameters, external futures and the choice of path, by variance-based sensitivity (Sobol indices on the ensemble) — so planners see which uncertainty more data can reduce and which only adaptation can.

3 · Signposts validated twice. Each early-warning signal is scored on held-out futures for lead time and false-alarm rate, then tested against dated events that happened — the electric share crossing thresholds, the 2019 Bergen vote, Oslo's 2019 restructuring.

03 · THE MODEL

What each agent decides and remembers

Acceptance is estimated in its core, cross-sectional form in A1, as an integrated choice and latent-variable (ICLV) model on real wave-1 data — the hybrid-choice approach used to read acceptance surveys elsewhere [hybridaccept] — extending Le Pira, Marcucci and Gatta from negotiating stakeholder groups to a population of individually estimated agents [lepira]. Each year t, agent n chooses a vehicle, how often to cross and when, from an estimated choice model where the instruments in force I(t) set the cost; acceptance follows a latent equation with one persistence term:

(1) U(n,j,t) = β·cost(j, I(t)) + θ'·x(n,j) + ε(n,j,t) (2) A*(n,t) = ρ·A(n,t−1) + δ·dose(n,t) + η·info(n) + γ'·z(n) + u(n,t) (3) pathway p admissible ⇔ share with A* ≥ λ holds every year; acceptance margin = largest rise in λ for which p stays admissible.

The coupling (DCM → ABM), in one loop. The estimated taste parameters β and θ are not re-tuned inside the simulation: each agent carries its own draw from the estimated distribution. In year t the agent reads the instruments I(t), computes utilities from (1), and chooses; its choice updates its fleet and its paid dose; (2) then carries acceptance into t+1 through ρ. So the ABM is not a DCM with a clock bolted on — the estimated behaviour is the agent, and memory makes last year's dose change this year's choice.

Backcasting, operationalised. Targets for 2050 are fixed first (below). The search does not run scenarios forward and keep the winners; it works backward: from the 2050 target set it asks which instrument states are admissible in 2049, then 2048, recursing to today, and keeps only sequences whose every step holds acceptance above λ and revenue above debt service. ρ demotes to a Level-3 extension (below); the core contribution does not depend on it. What would falsify it: if ρ≈0, memory is idle and claim 1 reduces to a static result; if η≈0, information does not move acceptance and H2 fails; if the fast engine cannot reproduce the network model within tolerance, the ensemble is cut to what the network model can run. Each is reported whichever way it falls — the stance is decision-making under deep uncertainty: we do not predict the future, we find paths robust across many.

the toll is a map, not a price

04 · CASE AND DESIGN

Kristiansund — where acceptance is live

Why Kristiansund, and where freight enters. Bypakke Kristiansund began collection on 1 June 2026 at two rv70 stations, Nordsundbrua and Omsundbrua, in both directions; about 70% of the package is toll-financed over up to 15 years [bypakke, innst337]. A car pays NOK 34, 27.20 with a tag, 19.04 if zero-emission with a tag [vegamot]. The council approved the basis 26–19 and the county 33–14: acceptance here is contested and live. The town sits on the Møre og Romsdal west-coast corridor, where the Ferjefri E39 programme and NGM both treat coastal short-sea shipping as the competing mode for freight to Oslo and the continent [ngm]. Freight is estimated here, not borrowed: a short carrier stated-choice block gives the mode-and-timing response of hauliers to a distance charge, which the NGM chains carry to the network. Because the heavy fleet turns over slowly and the sea alternative needs volume to stay viable, a pathway that charges road freight too late closes the modal-shift window and locks emissions in. The thesis reports the year the freight signpost must fire — and it is expected to fire before the passenger one, an expectation the design tests, not a result it assumes. Delete freight and that finding, and the earliest switch in the robust pathways, disappears. Molde, with no package, is the passenger comparison.

Survey — three blocks, Bayesian D-efficient

Acceptance (households): information randomised in three arms — personal cost as numbers, purpose as a short narrative, and control.

Passenger stated-choice: vehicle at replacement, crossing frequency and time, under tariff/discount levels inside national ranges.

Carrier stated-choice (firms/hauliers): road vs coastal short-sea, route and timing under a distance charge — single wave, small targeted sample. This is the estimated freight element; NGM-anchored for the network.

Kristiansund carries household + carrier; Molde the acceptance block. Wave 1 and the carrier block month 6; wave 2 month 18, same households. Planning sample n = 1,428 Kristiansund / 714 Molde, reported as minimum detectable effect.

Hypotheses · and a freight floor

H1 acceptance persists once a charge is permanent (ρ>0), within-person change between waves against dose.

H2 information moves acceptance independently of experience (η≠0), via the randomised arms — turning the Norwegian correlation [odeck08] into a causal estimate.

H3 a distance charge shifts carrier mode choice toward short-sea above a threshold rate.

Fallback. If the carrier sample under-powers, freight reverts to NGM elasticities with a reported sensitivity; the signpost is then a modelled consequence, not an estimate, and the thesis ladder absorbs it without losing a complete dissertation.

05 · PLAN, ROBUSTNESS, RECEIPTS

The robust front, and how it finishes in three years

The search, priced. A3 is a search, not a catalogue: it chooses the switch-on year and level of each instrument to maximise the share of 10³ futures meeting all three 2050 targets and minimise maximum regret, subject to acceptance ≥ λ every year, revenue ≥ debt service, and the accessibility floor — a many-objective robust problem solved with ε-NSGA-II over the ensemble, PRIM naming where the robust set still breaks [mordm, prim]. Treating robustness as the quantity to optimise under uncertainty follows the stability-metrics line of Shaabani, Hvattum, Hoff and Laporte [shaabani]. The search explores ~10⁴ candidate pathways (the enumerable core is 904 ordered instrument sequences, 4+36+216+648; the search widens that over switch-years and levels) against a Latin-hypercube sample of futures, the robust set re-checked on the full 10³: a few million fast-engine evaluations, ~40–150 CPU-hours, parallel. The fast engine's fidelity to the network model is measured, not assumed — on the real Molde and Kristiansund graphs it reproduces network travel times within ~15% (r 0.73–0.84) at three-to-four orders of magnitude less compute [evfid]; above tolerance, the future sample widens and the pathway set shrinks. A dynamic MATSim cross-check (Molde, 4,000 agents, 20 iterations) confirms it: ~19% vs MATSim at free flow (r 0.74), 6% static-vs-MATSim (r 0.999), and the free-flow engine correctly breaks under a tenfold congestion squeeze — hence the network re-check.
