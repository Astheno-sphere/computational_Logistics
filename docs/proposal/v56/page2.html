02 · FIT, GAP AND WHAT IS NEW

A dynamic layer beside the national models

The national system has three parts a candidate must name: NTM6, the national passenger model for trips over 70 km; RTM, the regional passenger model; and the national freight model, Nasjonal godsmodell (NGM), with its commodity-flow matrices and transport-chain costs [toi1819, ngm]. These anchor the National Transport Plan and are trusted for point appraisal. TØI’s forward-looking review recommends not replacing them but building dynamic models alongside them [toi1819]; the institute has begun, with a MATSim-based agent assignment for Greater Oslo, tramodsim [tramodsim]. This PhD joins that line. It supplies the exploratory, stress-testing layer that sequences instruments across thousands of futures and reports where each sequence fails, so the national models keep producing the appraisal the agencies rely on while this layer supplies the robustness and failure conditions they are not built to give.

The gap. Strategic models give end states and NGM gives freight chains; neither carries acceptance. Norwegian agent work assigns traffic with MATSim but takes acceptance as given [tramodsim]. Le Pira, Marcucci and Gatta integrated discrete choice and agent-based models for urban freight policy [lepira]; their agents are stakeholder groups negotiating in rounds, not a population living through calendar time. Adaptive pathway methods sequence actions against tipping points [haasnoot] but treat acceptance as an external scenario. The deep-uncertainty frontier runs ensembles over transport agent models, most recently on a fictional city with acceptance left out [fuhrer]. Nobody has estimated acceptance on real data, carried it in agents over calendar time, and let it decide which passenger and freight pathways survive.

The question beneath the whole proposal comes before any pathway: is public acceptance a measurable quantity that can be estimated on real data and carried forward in time, or not? The thesis answers that first; the ensemble and the robust front are what the answer is then used for.
1
Acceptance as an admissibility condition

A pathway counts only if estimated acceptance stays above a threshold at every step; the largest shift it survives is its acceptance margin, a result not an assumption.

2
Uncertainty decomposed by source

The spread in 2050 outcomes is split, year by year, into behavioural parameters, external futures and the choice of path, so planners see which more data can reduce.

3
Signposts validated twice, ordered across modes

Early-warning signals are scored on held-out futures for lead time and false alarms, then against dated events; passenger and freight signposts are compared.
