phd proposal · transport modelling for sustainability transitions · molde university college · transplan

The path, not the end state: household fleet simulation, estimated behaviour and exploratory backcasting for robust and acceptable low-emission transport

Arshad Akhtar Abbasia · PhD in Logistics, Molde University College · TRANSPLAN research areas 1 to 3
Case studies: Molde and Kristiansund, Møre og Romsdal · working prototype: github.com/Astheno-sphere/computational_Logistics

why now1 · The road, not the destination

Norway won the showroom. The fleet, the tax base and the politics have not caught up.

In 2025, 95.9% of new passenger cars sold in Norway were fully electric1, yet most cars on the road still burn fuel, and a car stays in the fleet for close to two decades. The fuel tax that financed roads is eroding. The 2027 budget proposes cutting the VAT exemption for electric cars to NOK 150,000, and the distance-based road charge meant to replace fuel taxes has stalled between the state and the municipalities2.

The state has asked for better methods. The National Transport Plan 2025 to 2036 commits to improving analytical methods so that the next plan can steer toward the 2050 low-emission society3, and the Ministry’s methods assignment admits that today’s models suit continued policy but are much less suited to showing what deep cuts require4. TRANSPLAN was created to answer this, starting from the 2050 goals and working backwards5.

TØI’s own review of models for backcasting names the limitation in one line: RTM and NTM6 compute partial equilibria but “say nothing about the path to a new equilibrium”6. Backcasting is a path. That path runs through households: the car each one owns, the year it is replaced, the trips it makes and the charges it will accept. My PhD builds the engine that gives TRANSPLAN that path, built from people’s estimated preferences, and tests whether the plans built on it survive the futures nobody can forecast.

what is new2 · Five contributions

Each claim is placed against the closest work found in the literature reviewed for this proposal.

C1 · THE PATH

A household engine that shows how Norway gets from today to 2050, year by year

Agents carry each household’s car, its age, its replacement moment, its mode choices and its response to price. Norway’s fleet model BIG follows vintages with an estimated choice model, but in aggregate and for fixed scenarios7.

nearest: Fridstrøm et al. 2016; Deuten et al. 2020
C2 · ACCEPTANCE THAT MOVES

Acceptance estimated from choice data, carried by agents, changing as a charge is lived, and used as a condition on which plans count

TØI’s national experiment found 70 to 80% support for moderate distance charges with earmarked revenue, with opposition among drivers with few alternatives and, partly, rural residents8. Support also rises after a charge is introduced9. Le Pira, Marcucci and Gatta built agents that carry acceptance for urban freight10; I carry it into passenger policy and into the robust search itself.

nearest: Lempert et al. 2020; Kasprzyk et al. 2013
C3 · HOW MUCH WE DO NOT KNOW

2050 uncertainty split into three parts, and a test of whether the winning policy survives each

Error in the estimated preferences, chance inside the simulation, and the deep uncertainty of the future are separated over 25 years. Petrik and colleagues propagated estimated parameters through a one-day activity model; parameter uncertainty exceeded sampling noise, with no long horizon and no policy choice11.

nearest: Petrik et al. 2016, 2020; for appraisal ranges, Tveter, Welde & Odeck 2025
C4 · WARNINGS WITH A HIT RATE

Backcast pathways stress-tested across thousands of futures, with signposts whose lead time and false-alarm rate are measured

Measured signpost detection appears mainly in water management12,13; transport backcasting remains national and deterministic14. Each signpost gets years of warning, false alarms and missed failures, and each pathway a measure of how often it must change.

nearest: Raso et al. 2019; Haasnoot et al. 2018
C5 · TESTED ON HISTORY

The engine is checked against Norway’s 2010 to 2025 electric transition before it is trusted with 2050

EV forecasts often fail against what happened15, and Norwegian traffic forecasts for fixed links missed long-run traffic by up to a factor of two24. Norway’s registration record allows the engine to be run from 2010 information and scored against the observed fleet.

nearest: Deuten et al. 2020 (system dynamics, 2010 to 2017)

where3 · Two towns, one street, one bridge

Molde and Kristiansund are case studies, not a national sample. They are car-dependent towns where national surveys find road charges hardest to accept, in a county whose 2025 climate accounts are not on track16, and where the decisive instruments (tolls, bus service, parking, cycling networks) are set locally. Their networks concentrate travel in ways that make policy effects visible. Molde is the main case, where preferences are estimated and the engine calibrated. Kristiansund is the transfer case: the Molde model is applied without re-estimation, which turns the transferability of preferences from an assumption into a measurement. Inputs come from national sources and outputs are reported against national targets, so the method carries to other regions.

how4 · One engine, three articles

The engine has four layers, and each article builds one of them on top of the layer below (plate 3). Each article is publishable on its own and hands a defined product to the next.

A1 · TRANSPLAN RA3 · YEAR 1

Preferences and acceptance

How do people in Molde and Kristiansund trade off time, cost and service, when do they replace their car, and which road-charging packages would they accept? Does acceptance measured nationally hold in car-dependent towns, and do preferences carry over from one town to the other?

Design. A stated-choice survey in both towns with three blocks: mode choice, timing and type of the next car, and votes on charging packages that vary charge level, exemptions, alternatives offered and how revenue is used. Attribute ranges follow the national experiment8 so the two can be compared. An efficient design is tested in a pilot of about 50 respondents; the full survey targets about 800 respondents with eight tasks each, a size checked in advance by recovering all six parameters of the prototype model within one standard error. Models. Mixed and latent-class logit, willingness to pay by segment, and an acceptance model with heterogeneity in trust and alternatives. Product. Estimated preferences with their covariance, and an acceptance function the agents can carry.

A2 · TRANSPLAN RA2 · YEAR 2

The household fleet engine

When estimated preferences, with their uncertainty, drive households with real cars, how do fleet renewal, mode shift, acceptance and who pays evolve year by year? Does the engine reproduce 2010 to 2025, and how much of its 2050 spread comes from preferences, from chance and from the future?

Engine. Households hold cars of known age and fuel, replace them by a choice model from A1, choose modes for their trips on the OpenStreetMap network, influence their neighbours, and form opinions on charges that shift with experience. The experience effect is calibrated on the Oslo field trial that accompanied the national acceptance experiment8, and peer influence is bounded by sensitivity analysis rather than claimed as identified. Preference parameters are drawn from their estimated distribution in every run. Validation. Calibration matches several observed patterns at once (mode shares from the national travel survey, fleet age and fuel from the vehicle register, traffic counts)17, with Kristiansund as a hold-out and the 2010 to 2025 transition as a historical test; the model is documented with the ODD protocol18. Emissions are counted well to wheel with Norwegian grid factors, including rebound in travel. The scope is commuting and other regular car travel by residents, the part of travel the survey and travel data can identify; results are reported both on that basis and translated to the national targets. Welfare is measured as consumer surplus from the estimated choice models, so it can be set beside Norwegian appraisal values, and zone data and trip lengths are taken from the regional model for the area (RTM) so the engine stays comparable with it. Product. A calibrated engine and an ensemble interface.

A3 · TRANSPLAN RA1 · YEAR 3

Robust pathways and warnings

Across thousands of futures, which packages stay effective, acceptable and affordable; when do they fail; and which reading in 2030 warns early, with what lead time and false-alarm rate?

Decision problem. For a package p over futures F: robustness R(p) is the share of futures in which commuting emissions in 2050 fall to τ of the base year; the search returns p* = argmax R(p) subject to acceptance A(p) ≥ ½ and public cost C(p) ≤ B, with regret and stability reported for comparison. Method. About 104 futures and 102 packages built from the instruments under discussion in 2026: distance-based road charging, toll rings in which electric cars pay, public transport frequency and fares, cycling infrastructure and fleet renewal. Futures are anchored on the strategies of the national methods work14 and screened for internal consistency. A many-objective robust search19 keeps packages that reach the target in most futures under two conditions: majority acceptance from the A2 agents, and a public budget. Scenario discovery finds the conditions under which packages fail20, Sobol indices rank the uncertainties, and a statistical surrogate replaces the engine if run time requires it. Signposts are scored for lead time, false alarms and missed failures12, and each pathway for how often it must change and what stability costs21. Product. Robust and acceptable pathways with measured signposts for Molde and Kristiansund.

A4 · OPTIONAL

Pathways that planners can agree on

A workshop with county and municipal planners and TRANSPLAN partners tests whether pathway stories help stakeholders agree on the next decision, building on storytelling for participatory planning22.

proof5 · It already runs

A working prototype implements the full chain on synthetic behaviour and the real road networks of both towns: two independent choice-model estimators with a recovery test, an agent model with fleet turnover, EV adoption, welfare and public money per commuter, and an ensemble of 60 futures by 25 packages. The behaviour is synthetic and its policy levers include instruments Norway is now phasing out, so the numbers show what each article will produce, not what will happen in Norway. Two results already point at the stock problem.

The code is public, the 87 automated tests run on every change, and every number in this proposal is rebuilt by committed scripts. The repository separates what runs, what is tested and what is planned, and lists its limitations.

Risks. A low survey response is met by combining a web panel with municipal channels, with the national travel survey as fallback. Delayed data access is covered by Statistics Norway commuting tables. Run time is handled by surrogates and national computing resources. Calibration that cannot be identified is constrained by several observed patterns at once and a hold-out town. Results that hinge on the target or the robustness metric are reported for targets of 10, 20 and 30% and compared across metrics. Ethics and data. The survey is assessed by Sikt before fieldwork, with informed consent and pseudonymised storage. Code is open under the MIT licence, and data are released with a DOI where consent allows.

what changes7 · For TRANSPLAN, the region and the field

For TRANSPLAN, a behavioural experiment for research area 3, a household engine for research area 2 that supplies the transition path national models lack, and measured pathways for the backcasting of research area 1, built to work alongside NTM6 and RTM and in the spirit of moving from “predict and provide” to “decide and provide”23. For Møre og Romsdal, tested pathways with signposts planners can monitor from traffic counts and the vehicle register. For the field, acceptance treated as a dynamic condition on robustness, uncertainty separated into its sources, and signposts with measured hit rates in transport. For logistics, the same engine and network tools carry to urban freight.

The fleet that will drive in 2035 is being bought now. This project builds the instrument to steer it, and to know early when a plan needs to change.
